The lottery draws 4 different numbers from the set \(S=\{1,2,\dots ,10\}\).  
Denote by \(k\) the number of her numbers that appear in the lottery’s draw.

\[
\begin{aligned}
k=4:&\quad\text{the draw equals }A ,\\[4pt]
k=3:&\quad\text{choose 3 of her 4 and 1 from the remaining 6 numbers},\\[4pt]
k=2:&\quad\text{choose 2 of her 4 and 2 from the remaining 6 numbers}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The number of possible draws is \(\binom{10}{4}=210\).

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
k & \text{Number of draws}  \\
\hline
4 & C(4,4)C(6,0)=1\\
3 & C(4,3)C(6,1)=4\cdot 6=24\\
2 & C(4,2)C(6,2)=6\cdot 15=90\\
1 & C(4,1)C(6,3)=4\cdot 20=80\\
0 & C(4,0)C(6,4)=15
\end{array}
\]

Thus

\[
P(\text{grand prize})=\frac{1}{210}, \qquad
P(\text{wins a prize})=\frac{1+24+90}{210}=\frac{115}{210}=\frac{23}{42}.
\]

The probability that she wins the grand prize given that she wins some prize is

\[
P(\text{grand prize}\mid \text{prize})
      =\frac{P(\text{grand prize})}{P(\text{wins a prize})}
      =\frac{1/210}{115/210}
      =\frac{1}{115}.
\]

This fraction is already in lowest terms, so \(m=1,\; n=115\) and

\[
m+n=1+115=116.
