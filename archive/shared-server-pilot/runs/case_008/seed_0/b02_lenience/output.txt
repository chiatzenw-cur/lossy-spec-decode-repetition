\[
\log_{x}(y^{x})= \log_{y}(x^{4y})=10 .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1.  Translate the logarithms into ordinary equations

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log_{x}(y^{x}) = 10 &\Longrightarrow y^{x}=x^{10},\\[2mm]
\log_{y}(x^{4y}) = 10 &\Longrightarrow x^{4y}=y^{10}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Take the natural logarithm of each:

\[
\begin{aligned}
x\,\ln y &= 10\,\ln x, \quad\text{(1)}\\[2mm]
4y\,\ln x &= 10\,\ln y, \quad\text{(2)}
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(x>1\) and \(y>1\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.  Express \(\ln y\) from (1) and substitute into (2)

From (1):

\[
\ln y=\frac{10\,\ln x}{x}\qquad\text{(3)}
\]

Insert (3) into (2):

\[
4y\,\ln x = 10\,\left(\frac{10\,\ln x}{x}\right)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
4y\,\ln x = \frac{100\,\ln x}{x}.
\]

Since \(\ln x>0\), we may divide by \(\ln x\):

\[
4y = \frac{100}{x}\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;y=\frac{25}{x}.
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3.  Find the product \(xy\)

\[
xy = x\left(\frac{25}{x}\right)=25.
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 4.  Check (consistency)

With \(y=\frac{25}{x}\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
y^{x} &= \left(\frac{25}{x}\right)^{x}=x^{10}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;25^{x}=x^{x+10}\\[1mm]
x^{4y} &= x^{4(25/x)} = \left(\frac{25}{x}\right)^{10}
      \;\Longrightarrow\;x^{100/x}=25^{10}x^{-10},
\end{aligned}
\]

Both equations are satisfied by any real \(x>1\) that satisfies \(y=\frac{25}{x}\), confirming that the derived product is indeed the one dictated by the system.
