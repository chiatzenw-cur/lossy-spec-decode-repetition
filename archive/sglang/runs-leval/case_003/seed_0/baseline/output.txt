This paper tackles simultaneous pruning and few‑bit quantization of deep nets by treating the task as variational Bayesian inference. The authors introduce a *quantizing prior*, a continuous relaxation of a multi‑spike‑and‑slab distribution that pulls weight posteriors toward a set of target values (e.g. ternary \(\{-r,0,r\}\)). By learning per‑weight dropout variances and means, the method produces a posterior that is either sharply concentrated at a quantization level or has large variance (hence prunable). The KL term is approximated by a lightweight, differentiable composition of the Sparse‑VD log‑uniform KL. Experiments on LeNet‑5 (MNIST) and DenseNet (CIFAR‑10) show that the network can be pruned and quantized without fine‑tuning, achieving comparable or better accuracy than several baselines.

**Strengths**

* The idea of a multi‑modal prior is elegant and directly enforces the desired quantization structure.
* The derivations are clear and the connection to MDL and Bayesian Occam’s razor is convincing.
* The proposed KL approximation is simple, fast, and reusable for any symmetric ternary codebook.
* Results demonstrate that significant sparsity can be obtained while keeping accuracy, and that the method works on both small and moderately large networks.

**Weaknesses & Questions**

1. **KL Approximation Accuracy**  
   The paper reports a maximum error of ~1 nat over the relevant \((\theta,\sigma)\) range, but the impact on final accuracy is unclear. Would a more accurate surrogate (e.g. a learned neural network) improve the pruning/quantization trade‑off?

2. **Quantization Levels**  
   Only ternary quantization is evaluated. How does the method generalize to 4‑ or 8‑bit codes? Does the same prior structure work when the number of modes grows?

3. **Hyper‑parameter Sensitivity**  
   Parameters such as \(\tau\), the clipping bounds, and the warm‑up schedule appear crucial. Ablation studies on these settings are missing. How robust is the method to different choices?

4. **Baseline Comparisons**  
   The paper compares mainly to pruning baselines and a few ternary methods. It would strengthen the contribution to include state‑of‑the‑art post‑training quantization approaches (e.g. LSQ, PACT) and Bayesian compression methods that do not use a multi‑modal prior.

5. **Computational Overhead**  
   The training cost relative to standard dropout or plain pruning is not quantified. Since the KL term is evaluated per weight, reporting training time and memory consumption would help practitioners assess feasibility.

6. **Generalization & Transfer**  
   The method is tested on a single architecture per dataset. Does VNQ transfer well to larger models or different domains (e.g. language models)?

**Suggestions**

* Provide ablations on the KL approximation and hyper‑parameters.
* Extend experiments to higher‑bit quantization levels and larger networks.
* Include a comparison to recent learned‑scale or learned‑threshold quantization baselines.
* Report training overhead and discuss potential optimizations (e.g. vectorized KL computation).

Overall, the paper presents a novel and well‑motivated approach to joint pruning and quantization. Addressing the above points would give a clearer picture of its practical impact and scalability.
