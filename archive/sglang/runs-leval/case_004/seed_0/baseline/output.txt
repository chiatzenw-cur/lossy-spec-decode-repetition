Network compression has become a central research line as deep models grow in size. Two dominant techniques – pruning and quantization – aim to reduce parameters while preserving accuracy. Early works used hand‑crafted sparsity, but recent efforts learn compression directly from data.  

Bayesian compression offers a principled alternative to deterministic pruning. Louizos et al. (2017) introduced a sparsity‑inducing prior that yields a posterior over weight magnitudes, enabling automatic removal of unimportant parameters. Neklyudov et al. (2017) extended this to structured pruning by using log‑normal multiplicative noise to prune whole neurons or filters.  

Variational dropout has been adapted for sparsification. Molchanov et al. (2017) proposed Sparse Variational Dropout, learning a dropout rate for each weight and pruning those with high uncertainty. It matches or exceeds deterministic baselines while providing a Bayesian posterior that captures weight uncertainty.  

Soft weight sharing clusters weight values into a small set of centroids via a mixture prior. Ullrich et al. (2017) showed that this clustering prior dramatically reduces the effective number of parameters while maintaining accuracy, and can be combined with variational inference for a compact posterior.  

Trained ternary quantization (Zhu & Han, 2016) learns three discrete weight values and shows that ternary networks can rival full‑precision accuracy on ImageNet. This motivates a multi‑modal prior that pulls weights toward a few quantization levels, as we propose.  

Bayesian treatments also provide uncertainty estimates. Gal (2016) showed dropout can be viewed as approximate Bayesian inference, yielding calibrated uncertainty. Our method shares this Bayesian foundation, enabling both compression and uncertainty quantification in one framework.  

The KL divergence term in the ELBO controls the trade‑off between fit and sparsity. Molchanov et al. (2017) provided a closed‑form approximation for a zero‑centered log‑uniform prior, which we extend to our multi‑modal quantizing prior. This lets us train efficiently while concentrating the posterior around the desired quantization levels.
