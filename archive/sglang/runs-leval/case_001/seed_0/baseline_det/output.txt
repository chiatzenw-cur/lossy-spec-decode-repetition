**Summary**  
The paper investigates regularization techniques (L1/L2, dropout, BN, weight clipping, entropy) for policy‑gradient RL algorithms (A2C, TRPO, PPO, SAC) on MuJoCo and RoboSchool continuous‑control tasks. It claims simple L2 decay often outperforms the standard entropy bonus and that dropout/BN help only off‑policy methods. This study is among the first to systematically evaluate such regularizers in deep RL, addressing a gap where training tricks are often overlooked.

**Strengths**  
Strengths include a large experimental sweep: six regularizers, four algorithms, nine tasks, and robustness tests over hyper‑parameters, network sizes, and policy‑vs‑value ablation. The authors report mean, std, and rank statistics, providing a solid empirical foundation and high‑confidence statistical tests for the reported trends. They also compare against entropy regularization, a staple in policy optimization, and present clear rank‑based summaries that aid in interpreting the relative merits of each method.

**Limitations**  
However, the work has limitations. The scope is limited to continuous‑control domains, leaving discrete or sparse‑reward problems untested. Hyper‑parameter tuning is per‑environment, which may bias the reported optimal strengths. The paper offers little theoretical insight into why dropout and BN benefit only off‑policy methods, and why entropy can be dominated by simple L2 decay. Moreover, five random seeds may be insufficient for high‑variance tasks such as HalfCheetah.

**Questions**  
Questions: Why does dropout fail for TRPO but not for PPO or A2C? Did the authors try different dropout rates or mask placements? The paper states BN hurts on‑policy methods – is this due to rapidly changing state distributions? How is entropy added to SAC, which already optimizes for entropy, and does the additional penalty conflict with the learned temperature? Would sample‑efficiency curves or learning‑curve slopes provide a clearer picture of regularization benefits?

**Suggestions**  
To strengthen the study, the authors should broaden the benchmark to discrete actions and sparse‑reward settings, and release a grid‑search or Bayesian‑optimized hyper‑parameter distribution to show robustness. A brief theoretical or empirical analysis of the interaction between entropy and L2 penalties would clarify when they can be combined. Reporting learning‑curve slopes or sample‑efficiency curves would demonstrate practical gains beyond final return. Finally, providing the full code and hyper‑parameter tables would greatly enhance reproducibility and adoption.

**Conclusion**  
The paper offers a valuable empirical survey, but addressing the above points will increase its impact and usefulness to the RL community. In particular, a more diverse experimental set, deeper analysis of regularization dynamics, and open‑source artifacts would transform this study into a reference for practitioners aiming to squeeze extra performance from existing policy‑optimization algorithms in practice.
