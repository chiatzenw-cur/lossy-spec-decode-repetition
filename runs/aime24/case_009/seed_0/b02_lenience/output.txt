Let a position with \(n\) tokens be **winning** if the player to move can force a win,  
and **losing** otherwise.  
Let

\[
P(n)=\begin{cases}
\text{True}, &\text{if }n \text{ is winning},\\[1mm]
\text{False}, &\text{if }n \text{ is losing}.
\end{cases}
\]

We use \(1\) or \(4\) tokens per move.

---

### 1.  Recurrence

\(P(0)=\text{False}\) (no move possible).  
For \(n\ge 1\)

\[
P(n)=\begin{cases}
\text{True},  &\text{if }\, P(n-1)=\text{False}\text{ or }\; P(n-4)=\text{False},\\
\text{False}, &\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

The rest of the table is obtained by simple, successive evaluations.

---

### 2.  Computation of the first few values

| \(n\) | \(P(n)\) | Comment |
|------|----------|---------|
| 0 | F | losing |
| 1 | T | move 1 to 0 |
| 2 | F | only 1→1, all winning |
| 3 | T | 1→2 (losing) |
| 4 | T | 4→0 (losing) |
| 5 | F | 1→4 (win), 4→1 (win) |
| 6 | T | 1→5 (losing) , 4→2 (losing) |
| 7 | F | 1→6 (win), 4→3 (win) |
| 8 | T | 1→7 (losing) |
| 9 | T | 4→5 (losing) |
|10 | F | 1→9 (win), 4→6 (win) |
|11 | T | 1→10 (losing) |

|13 | T | 1→12 (losing) |
|14 | T | 4→10 (losing) |

|… | … | … |

From the table we see that the losing positions are

\[
0,\ 2,\ 5,\ 7,\ 10,\ 12,\ 15,\ 17,\ \ldots
\]

The pattern of differences is
\[
+2,\; +3,\; +2,\; +3,\; +2,\; +3,\dots
\]

Hence the numbers that are losing for the player to move are exactly those congruent
to \(0\) or \(2\pmod 5\).

**Proof by induction**

*Base:*  
For \(n=0,2,5,7\) we already computed \(P(n)=\text{False}\).

*Induction step:*  
Assume all numbers \(<k\) satisfy \(P(n)=\text{False}\) iff \(n\equiv 0\text{ or }2\pmod 5\).
Consider \(k\).

* If \(k\equiv 0\text{ or }2\pmod 5\) then  
  \(k-1\equiv 4\text{ or }1\pmod 5\) and \(k-4\equiv 1\text{ or }3\pmod 5\);  
  both are winning by the induction hypothesis, thus \(P(k)=\text{False}\).

* If \(k\not\equiv 0,2\pmod 5\) we have at least one move
  from \(k\) to \(k-1\) (if \(k\equiv 1\text{ or }4\) mod 5) or \(k-4\) (if \(k\equiv 3\) mod 5).
  The destination lies in \(\{0,2\}\pmod 5\) and is a losing position.
  Therefore \(P(k)=\text{True}\).

Thus the property holds for all \(n\).

---

### 3.  Who can guarantee a win?

Alice moves first.  
Bob can force a win at the start iff the initial position is a **losing** one for the player to move, i.e. iff

\[
n\equiv 0 \pmod 5 \quad\text{or}\quad n\equiv 2 \pmod 5.
\]

---

### 4.  Counting \(n\le 2024\)

* Numbers \(\equiv 0\pmod 5\) and \(1\le n\le 2024\):  
  \(5,10,15,\dots,2020\)  
  Count \(= \left\lfloor \frac{2024}{5}\right\rfloor = 404\).

* Numbers \(\equiv 2\pmod 5\) and \(1\le n\le 2024\):  
  \(2,7,12,\dots,2022\)  
  Count \(= \left\lfloor \frac{2024-2}{5}\right\rfloor + 1
              = \left\lfloor \frac{2022}{5}\right\rfloor +1
              = 404+1 = 405\).
