Everyone’s view of time and mortality is a moving target, not a fixed value. As we grow, we recruit new mental schemas, new emotional filters, and new social roles—each reshaping the way we compute, interpret, and feel about the clock and the urn. Below are the main “stages” of life that most commonly reorder our understanding of time and mortality, along with some evidence that explains why we feel the way we do.

---

## 1. **Early childhood (0‑6 yrs) – the “eternal now”**

| What we experience | What we think about time | What we feel about mortality |
|--------------------|--------------------------|------------------------------|
| **Cyclical, sensory‑rich world** | Minutes feel like waves; the “future” is a vague shape. | **Myth of immortality**: “I can stay in this place forever.” |
| **Concrete & vivid learning** | Time is “seen” by the rhythm of glass‑cylinders, e.g., the time a light stays lit. | **No tangible threat**: the idea of “die” is an abstract concept (e.g., “the black smoke” of the stove). |

### Why it happens

- **Cognitive stage**: Babies are in a world of *“now”*—they have no pre‑emptive concept of “later” or “later.”  
- **Social protective**: Parents are always “in the space” to avert harm, so mortality is one component of *“protecting”*.

## 2. **Middle childhood (7‑12 yrs) – “the countdown”**

| What we experience | What we think about time | What we feel about mortality |
|--------------------|--------------------------|------------------------------|
| **School and schedules** | The *“minutes”* of a test start noticing the self‑law of time being a *“good teacher.”* | **Uncertainty about risk**: “I’ll stay in the park until I die.” |
| **Narrative frameworks** | Books and cartoons teach that time is *“linear”*—you can meet a time paradox. | **First tinges of vulnerability**: “When I’m older, I’ll be bad.” |

### Why it happens

- **Cognitive stage**: Piaget calls this *“pre‑operations”*—the world is formatted as it is; the bigger the timetable, the more “graded” it becomes.  
- **Social/role playing**: reliability grows with children’s maturity; many are still “under control” of the adults.

### Why it happens

- **Erikson’s psychosocial stage**: *Identity vs. role confusion* – a crucial nexus. The “life” distance from death is now a part of a *self‑concept* that blurs *binary* with *finiteness*.
