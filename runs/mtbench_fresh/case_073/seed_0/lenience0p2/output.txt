## 1.  Antitrust Principles & Their Impact on Market Competition

1. **“Monopolies hurt consumers”** – In both countries the underlying principle is that a company that can set prices to a monopoly level or that can block competitors from participating in a market ultimately harms product/service quality and consumer prices.  This belief is embedded in the **Consumer‑Welfare** principle.

2. **Competitive remedies** – Structured “cross‑price” or “rule‑of‑reason” analysis in the US prohibits exclusionary or predatory practices while allowing “product‑quality” and “market‑efficiency” benefits.  The Chinese authorities generally apply a “non‑corruption” or “competency” measure: business in which it has market power can allegations.

3. **Pro‑competitive policy** – The US tests “anti‑competitive” conduct by allowing “negligible consumer harm” or “non‑monopolistic” outcome.  China tests the *market value* to denounce or restrict the offending behavior.

4. **Market forces** – In both countries, competition is defined by *entry barriers*.  The bigger the threats to entry, the higher the enforcement importance.  The US tends to keep monopolies “harmless” in the sense of allowing them to stay if they are beneficial for consumers.  China’s leader state emphasises that a market with monopoly but *association* to the state would create unrealistic monopolies.

5. **Intellectual & data** – Digital and “tech‑heavy” sectors have seen a rise in antitrust scrutiny.  The US has led the development in “real‑world” data‑driven monopolies—Google, Amazon, Facebook, Apple.
