### How it works in a nutshell

1. **Light‑dependent reactions**  
   * Photons excite electrons in the pigments of Photosystem II (PSII) and later in Photosystem I (PSI).  
   * The excited electrons are transferred through a series of carriers (plastoquinone, cytochrome b₆f, plastocyanin, and PSI) while protons are pumped into the thylakoid lumen, creating a proton motive force used to synthesize ATP.  
   * Water molecules are split (photolysis) to replenish the electrons that PSII loses, producing free oxygen (O₂) as a by‑product.  
   * Electrons are ultimately reduced to NADPH by the enzyme NADP⁺ reductase.

2. **Light‑independent reactions (Calvin Cycle)**  
   * CO₂ is fixed (carboxylated) by RuBisCO, forming a 3‑carbon molecule, 3‑phosphoglycerate (3‑PGA).  
   * ATP provides the energy needed for the phosphorylation of 3‑PGA, while NADPH donates the reducing power to convert it to glyceraldehyde‑3‑phosphate (G3P).  
   * Some G3P is used to regenerate the CO₂‑carrier RuBP, enabling the cycle to continue.  
   * The remaining G3P is used (with other enzyme reactions) to synthesize glucose and other carbohydrates, which are carried out of the chloroplast and utilized by the plant.

**Bottom‑line**: In the chloroplast, the “light” or “photoreactive” part is the war‑zone of the thylakoid membranes, turning energy and water into ATP, NADPH, and O₂; the “dark” or “Calvin” part is the stroma, using the ATP and NADPH as fuel to lock CO₂ into sugars that sustain life.
