Answer.<|end|><|start|>assistant<|channel|>final<|message|>The problem is essentially asking for the probability that the sum of the two fair numbers is **≥ 3**.

---

### 1.  Total possible outcomes  

When \(l\) dice are rolled, each die can be any of \(\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}\).  
There are  
\[
2\cdot 6 = 36
\]
ordered outcomes for the two dice.  

---

### 2.  Outcomes that give a sum **≤ 2**  

The only way to get a sum that is **not** at least 3 is to get a sum of 2:

* \(1+1 = 2\) – this is the only outcome that gives a sum of 2.

Therefore the only possible “bad” outcome is \((1,1)\).

---

### 3.  Count the “good” outcomes  

\[
\text{Good outcomes}= 36 \;-\; 1 = 35
\]

---

### 4.  Probability  

\[
P(\text{sum}\ge 3)=\frac{\text{Good outcomes}}{\text{Total outcomes}}
   = \frac{35}{36}\approx 0.9723
\]

---

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle P(\text{sum at least }3)=\frac{35}{36}\approx0.9723}
\]
