\[
\begin{aligned}
&\theta_1=15^\circ , \qquad
\theta_2=30^\circ , \qquad
\theta_3=45^\circ ,\\[2mm]
&\theta_4=60^\circ ,\qquad
\theta_5=75^\circ ,\qquad
\theta_6=90^\circ .
\end{aligned}
\]

parallel sides must lie on two lines that are as with the perpendicular

\((V_{0},V_{2}),\,(V_{2},V_{4}),\dots\)…  

For a fixed perpendicular pair of lines we obtain exactly one rectangle
per choice of all four *L***–points, thus we obtain

\[
2\times 6 = 6 = 12
\]

rectangles for the \(k=1\) case.

Do the same for the pair \((60^\circ ,150^\circ)\); there are
\(6\) areas of same orientation, giving another \(6\) rectangles.

#### The pair \((90^\circ ,180^\circ)\)

With the orientation that is perpendicular to 90°, the ‘90°’ lines correspond
to a single straight line through the centre; each perpendicular line is
a horizontal line through the polygon.  
There again make the two triangles from the diagonals, each such configuration
creates one rectangle.  
There are \(4\) such triangles among the sides, giving

\[
4
\]

rectangles.

### 4.  Total

Adding the contributions from the three sets,

\[
6+6+12 = 24 .
