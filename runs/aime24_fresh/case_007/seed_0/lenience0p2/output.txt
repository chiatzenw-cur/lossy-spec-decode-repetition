For a box of side‑lengths \(a,b,c\) the smallest sphere that can contain the box is the
sphere centred at the centre of the box, whose radius is half the length of the space
diagonal.  
Hence

\[
r_{\text{box}}=\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}}{2} .
\]

We need a sphere that can contain **every** box in \(\mathcal{B}\).  
Let  

\[
r=\max_{(a,b,c)\text{ satisfying the two constraints}}
\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}}{2},
\]

i.e. \(r^{2}\) is the maximum of \(\dfrac{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}{4}\).

---

### Maximising \(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}\)

Set  

\[
p=a+b+c,\qquad 
q=ab+bc+ca=27,\qquad 
s=abc=23 .
\]

Then

\[
a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=p^{2}-2q = p^{2}-54 .
\]

Thus the problem is to maximise \(p^{2}\).

---

#### Using Lagrange multipliers

Let

\[
F(a,b,c)=a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2},
\quad
g(a,b,c)=ab+bc+ca-27,\quad
h(a,b,c)=abc-23.
\]

Set  

\[
\nabla F = \lambda \nabla g + \mu \nabla h .
\]

This gives

\[
2a=\lambda(b+c)+\mu bc,\qquad
2b=\lambda(c+a)+\mu ac,\qquad
2c=\lambda(a+b)+\mu ab .
\]

From the symmetry we deduce that at an extremum either  

* \(a=b\neq c\) or  
* the three numbers are all different.

Trying \(a=b=x\) we find \(c=23/x^{2}\).  
Using the constraint \(q=27\) we obtain

\[
x^{2}+2x\frac{23}{x^{2}}=27
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x^{3}-27x+46=0 .
\]

The cubic factors as  

\[
(x-2)(x^{2}+2x-23)=0 ,
\]

yielding

\[
\boxed{(a,b,c)=(2,\,2,\,\tfrac{23}{4})}
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\boxed{(a,b,c)=( -1+2\sqrt6,\; -1+2\sqrt6,\; \tfrac{23}{( -1+2\sqrt6)^2}) } .
\]

The latter gives a smaller sum of squares, so the maximum is achieved by the first
triple:

\[
a=b=2,\qquad c=\frac{23}{4}.
\]

Now

\[
a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=4+4+\left(\frac{23}{4}\right)^{2}
=8+\frac{529}{16}
=\frac{657}{16}.
\]

Hence

\[
r^{2}=\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}{4}
=\frac{657}{64}.
\]

Thus \(r^{2}\) is already in lowest terms \(\displaystyle \frac{657}{64}\).

Finally

\[
p+q=657+64=721.
\]
