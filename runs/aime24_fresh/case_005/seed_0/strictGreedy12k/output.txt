If \(p\) is an odd prime, the multiplicative group \(\mathbb F_{p}^{\times}\) is cyclic of order
\(p-1\).  
Let \(g\) be a generator.  The congruence

\[
x^{4}\equiv -1 \pmod p
\]

has a solution iff there exists \(k\) such that  

\[
4k\equiv \frac{p-1}{2}\pmod{\,p-1\,},
\]

which is solvable exactly when \(\gcd(4,p-1)\mid (p-1)/2\).
Writing \(p-1=2^{\alpha}m\;(m\ \text{odd})\) shows that the above holds
iff \(\alpha \ge 3\), i.e. \(p-1\) is divisible by \(8\).

Thus the smallest prime satisfying the condition is the least prime
\(p\equiv 1\pmod 8\).

\[
p=17.
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### Lifting the solution modulo \(p^{2}\)

We need \(n^{4}+1\equiv 0 \pmod{17^{2}}\).
First solve modulo \(17\):

\[
2^{4}=16\equiv -1\pmod{17}
\]

so \(n\equiv 2\pmod{17}\) is one solution.
Let \(f(x)=x^{4}+1\) and \(f'(x)=4x^{3}\).
Since \(f'(2)=4\cdot 2^{3}=32\equiv 15\not\equiv 0\pmod{17}\),
by Hensel’s lemma there is a unique lift of \(n\equiv2 \pmod{17}\)
to a solution modulo \(17^{2}=289\).

Write a lift as \(n=2+17k\).  Modulo \(289\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
(2+17k)^{4}
&\equiv 2^{4}+4\cdot2^{3}\cdot17k \pmod{289}\\
&=16+544k .
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
16+544k+1\equiv0\pmod{289}\iff 17+255k\equiv0\pmod{289}.
\]

Since \(255=289-34=17\cdot15\) and \(289=17\cdot17\),

\[
15k\equiv16\pmod{17}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad k\equiv 9\pmod{17}.
\]

Hence the lift is  

\[
n=2+17k=2+17\cdot9=155.
\]
