### Probability of winning a prize

A prize is won when at least two of her numbers are among the lottery’s numbers.  
Let \(M\) be the number of matches, \(M\ge 2\).

\[
\begin{aligned}
P(M=2) &= \frac{\binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}}{\binom{10}{4}}
          = \frac{6\cdot 15}{210}
          = \frac{90}{210}=\frac{3}{7},\\[4pt]
P(M=3) &= \frac{\binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}}{\binom{10}{4}}
          = \frac{4\cdot 6}{210}
          = \frac{24}{210}=\frac{4}{35},\\[4pt]
P(M=4) &= \frac{\binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}}{\binom{10}{4}}
          = \frac{1}{210}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus  

\[
P(M\ge 2)=\frac{90+24+1}{210}= \frac{115}{210}= \frac{23}{42}.
\]

---

### Conditional probability

\[
P(M=4\mid M\ge 2)=\frac{P(M=4)}{P(M\ge 2)}
=\frac{1/210}{23/42}
=\frac{42}{210\cdot 23}
=\frac{42}{4830}
=\frac{7}{805}
=\frac{1}{115}.
\]

So the probability is \(m/n=1/115\), where \(m=1\) and \(n=115\).
