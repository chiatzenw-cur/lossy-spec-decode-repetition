Let Jen’s chosen set be fixed.  
The number of possible 4‑number draws is  

\[
\binom{10}{4}=210.
\]

For a given \(k\) (number of matches with Jen’s set), the number of draws that match exactly \(k\) numbers is  

\[
\binom{4}{k}\binom{6}{4-k},
\]
because we pick \(k\) of Jen’s \(4\) numbers and \(4-k\) of the other \(6\) numbers.

---

### Probability of a grand prize

All four numbers match:
\[
\binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}=1.
\]
So  

\[
P(\text{grand prize})=\frac{1}{210}.
\]

---

### Probability of at least two matches

\[
\begin{aligned}
k=2:&\ \binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}=6\cdot15=90,\\[4pt]
k=3:&\ \binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}=4\cdot6=24,\\[4pt]
k=4:&\ \binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Adding, the number of draws with at least two matches is  

\[
90+24+1=115.
\]

Hence

\[
P(\text{at least two matches})=\frac{115}{210}.
\]

---

### Conditional probability

\[
P(\text{grand prize} \mid \text{at least two matches})=
\frac{P(\text{grand prize})}{P(\text{at least two matches})}
= \frac{\dfrac{1}{210}}{\dfrac{115}{210}}
= \frac{1}{115}.
\]

This fraction is already in lowest terms, so with \(m=1\) and \(n=115\),

\[
m+n=1+115=116.
\]
