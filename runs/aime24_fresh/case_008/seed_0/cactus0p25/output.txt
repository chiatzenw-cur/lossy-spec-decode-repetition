\log_x(y^{x})&=10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\ln (y^{x})}{\ln x}=10
        &&\Longrightarrow\quad x\ln y=10\ln x\tag{1}\\[4pt]
\log_y(x^{4y})&=10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\ln (x^{4y})}{\ln y}=10
        &&\Longrightarrow\quad 4y\ln x=10\ln y\tag{2}
\end{aligned}
\]

Because \(x>1\) and \(y>1\), \(\ln x>0\) and \(\ln y>0\).  
Solve (1) for \(\ln y\):

\[
\ln y = \frac{10\ln x}{x}.
\]

Solve (2) for \(\ln y\):

\[
\ln y=\frac{4y\ln x}{10}= \frac{2\,y\ln x}{5}.
\]

Equating the two expressions for \(\ln y\):

\[
\frac{10\ln x}{x} = \frac{2y\ln x}{5}\;\Longrightarrow\; 
\frac{10}{x} = \frac{2y}{5}\;\Longrightarrow\;
xy = \frac{10}{2}\times5=25.
\]

Thus the product of the two numbers is

\boxed{25}.
