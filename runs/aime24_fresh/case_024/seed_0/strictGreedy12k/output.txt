### 1.  Equation from reading rows

\[
(100a+10b+c)+(100d+10e+f)=999 .
\]

Adding digitwise with possible carries \(c_1,c_2\) from the unit and ten
positions,

\[
\begin{aligned}
c+f &= 9+10c_1,\\
b+e+c_1&=9+10c_2,\\
a+d+c_2 &=9 .
\end{aligned}
\]

All numbers are single digits, so \(c_1,c_2\in\{0,1\}\) and the left–hand
sides are at most \(18\).  
Thus \(c_1\) cannot be \(1\) (otherwise \(c+f=19>18\)), giving

\[
c_1=0,\qquad c+f=9 .
\]

With \(c_1=0\), the second equation forces \(c_2=0\)
(because if \(c_2=1\) we would need \(b+e=19\), impossible).  
Hence

\[
b+e=9,\qquad a+d=9 .
\tag{1}
\]

### 2.  Equation from reading columns

Reading each column top‑to‑bottom gives two‑digit numbers:
\(\overline{ad},\,\overline{be},\,\overline{cf}\).
Their sum is

\[
10(a+b+c)+(d+e+f)=99. \tag{2}
\]

Using (1) we find \(d=9-a,\;e=9-b,\;f=9-c\).  
Substituting into (2),

\[
10(a+b+c)+(27-(a+b+c))=99
\Longrightarrow a+b+c=8.
\]

With this, (1) is automatically satisfied and each complementary digit
belongs to \(\{0,\dots ,9\}\).  
Thus all grids are uniquely determined by the choice of
\(a,b,c\) such that

\[
a,b,c\ge0,\qquad a+b+c=8 .
\]

The number of such non‑negative integer solutions equals

\[
\binom{8+3-1}{3-1}
=\binom{10}{2}
=45 .
\]

Every such choice gives a distinct arrangement that satisfies both
conditions, and no other arrangements exist.
